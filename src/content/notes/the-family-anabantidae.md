The family Anabantidae also known as the Betas, Gourami's, and paradise fish is a small family of fishes with only 33 species officially described by science. They are found native to Africa and Asia. The closest known marine relative of the family Anabantidae is the species in the order Carangiformes (jacks, trevallies, and pompano).  Members of the Anabantidae family  possess a labyrinth organ which acts as a lung allowing Anabantidae species to "breath" air.   Their bodies  have evolved rough ctenoid scales that allow them to travel on damp ground to find water sources.  This combination of adaptions have allowed them to thrive in shallow, stagnant, and low oxygen water bodies such as bogs, swamps, and rice paddies.

     In the home aquarium examples of iconic Anabantidae are the pearl gourami, the betta, the honey gourami, the three spot gourami, and finally the paradise fish. The smallest known Anabantidae in the world is Nanosphronemus gemma which reaches a maximum size of 18 mm. The largest member of this family is the Gangetic Koi also known by its scientific name Anabas Cobojius which reaches a maximum length of 11.8 inches.  The defining characteristic of this family is the labyrinth organ that allows gourami, Betta, and paradise fish to rise to the surface and breath in air.  The Labrynth organ is acts as a lung and veins allow for oxygen exchange with its blood stream.  The second characteristic of Anabantidae is that they are equipped with rough ctenoid scales that allow them to gain mechanical strength and protect them from predation by acting like a rough physical barrier against predators and abrasion.   The third characteristic in common with this family is the presence of spiny gill covers that flare out when predators try to swallow them making them difficult to swallow.  It also allows members of the family to anchor themselves on the ground when moving over land in search of water.  The fourth characteristic of the anabantidae are teeth on the parasphenoid bone that allows them to grip hard shelled or strong clawed prey inside the oral cavity.
