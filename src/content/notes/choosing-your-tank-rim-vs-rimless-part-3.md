Today tanks are very commonly sold in one of two principle constructions.  Those constructions are rimmed and rimless tanks.  Neither of these tanks are inherently superior to one another.  The truth is when constructed appropriately, both tanks are more then capable of reliably serving the needs of the hobbyist.  Though a hobbyist can find a high degree of success with both types of tanks, there are situations where one type of tanks is favored over another because of the advantages that it offers.   Finally there are situations outside of the hobby where one type of aquarium offers advantages over the other.  This article will examine  both types and the advantages and disadvantages of both.  However understand that for the same volumes strictly speaking both types of aquaria are equally able to serve the hobbyists needs.

     We are going to start with the most traditional form of aquarium.  That form is the rimmed aquarium.  The rimmed aquarium usually has two rims on it.  The first is located at the bottom of the tank and the second lines the top of the panels.   These panels are usually grooved in such a way that the bottom rim is where the base panel sits upon.  The bottom rim is designed to take the pressure of the mass of the entire tank, reinforce structurally the bottom of the tank to prevent bowing, and finally distribute the force of the mass among the rim points that the aquarium sits upon.  The top rim of the tank has two main functions as well.  The first function is it is the point where the lid can fit securely upon.  The rim itself is usually grooved in such a way that the glass fits securely into it.  The second function is that it structurally reinforces the glass panel.   This prevents bowing at the top and reduces the pressure placed upon the silicon sealant from the force of the mass of the water pushing against the panels.  The top rim accomplishes this by holding the aquarium together.  The structural supports are so important  that many movers will tell you that moving with a rimless tank is very risky as many of those tanks end up failing after the move.   Rimmed tanks on the other hand have a much reduced rate of failure when set up immediately upon settling down from a move.   This is their principle advantage, the structural support that they provide makes them more durable and more resiliant with a reduced rate of structural failure.
